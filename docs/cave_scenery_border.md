# Visual cave scenery border — Stage 1

The logical map remains 42×32. `DungeonRenderer.cave_scenery_border_width` defaults to 6; setting it to 0 restores the previous filler footprint and removes the distant apron. The existing terrain, height variation, cap rotation, and materials remain the fallback. No tile/elevation/spawn/traversal, collision, navigation, camera, sprite, or turn-system changes were made.

Files changed: `scripts/dungeon/cave_terrain_backdrop.gd`, `scripts/dungeon/dungeon_renderer.gd`, new `scripts/dungeon/cave_scenery_apron.gd`, `tests/validate_cave_background.gd`, new `tests/validate_cave_scenery.gd`, this document, and `docs/CODEX_HANDOFF.md` (plus the new script UID generated by Godot).

## Geometry and visibility

`CaveTerrainBackdrop.plan()` can grow a **visual** rectangle independently of `DungeonData.map_bounds`. The distance/clearance calculation continues through that rectangle, using existing coordinate-hashed full/half rock heights and actual structural protection. Interior records are unchanged. Existing outside-boundary filler is respected rather than duplicated. Border records are marked `external`; they have no discovery state and never illuminate because of logical fog discovery. Their global dim/other-elevation palette matches the surrounding background.

Detailed scenery continues six cells beyond each logical edge. Six alone is insufficient when looking past an elevated edge: the narrow perspective can see substantially farther down to ground. `CaveSceneryApron` adds four distant plane strips beyond the detailed scenery, using one shared material and eight triangles. It computes a conservative guard from the actual camera corner rays, maximum walkable surface height, and ground base, plus two cells of safety. The default camera yields an 18-cell total visual margin. This keeps the **outermost** scenery boundary outside normal camera views without extending detailed rock instances across that whole margin. The strips do not cover the playable interior or fill bridge air space.

The apron is static. Its four strips rebuild only during setup or viewport resize. There are no per-frame terrain scans, moving backgrounds, atmospheric shaders, alpha fades, new collision, or new shadow casters. Its simple distant surface is intentionally quiet; continuous geometry/material experiments are later stages.

Ordinary fog refreshes visit the existing interior record indices only. External colors change only when the global elevation palette changes, with one corresponding apron material update. No additional border cells are discovered or added to the minimap. Foundations, terraces, shallow bridge supports, ramps and normal half-height walls retain their original geometry.

## Architecture recommendation for Stage 2 review

Start with an optional **ground-only chunk ArrayMesh** driven by `CaveTerrainBackdrop.records`, retaining the current MultiMesh mode for A/B. Keep terrain heights/clearance decisions in the existing planner. Do not merge foundations, ramps, lower walls or bridge supports into that mesh.

- Merge adjacent coplanar tops only when height and fog/tint treatment are compatible. Coordinate tint differences constrain merging; a later world-space stone material can replace tile-based tint only after visual review.
- Omit shared side faces when a neighboring filled ground volume covers them. Preserve exposed sides and the legitimate air beneath shallow supports. Do not infer solid causeways from upper walkable cells.
- Chunk meshes can use indexed ArrayMesh or SurfaceTool construction at floor setup. Fog color changes should update color attributes or a small shader palette on existing chunks when logical fog changes; rebuilding geometry or rescanning scene nodes every frame would undo the current performance work.
- Existing `fog_visuals` walls/foundations/terraces fade or hide individually. Merging them across ownership/occlusion boundaries requires a separate design, so exclude them from the first prototype.
- For Stage 3, test a shared world-space triplanar stone material on the prototype ground and then compatible cliff materials. Reuse deterministic textures, keep normal/roughness detail subtle, preserve fog/shadow/opacity behavior, and retain the three recognizable foundation layers. Current source assets mostly use modeled detail and colors, so texture mapping alone will not remove cubic geometry.
- For Stage 4, first weather exposed decorative cliff faces with anchored joins. Any bevel/displacement must stay inside decorative footprints and away from ramps/walkable clearances. Preserve full required foundation coverage and bridge depth. Avoid smoothing topology/elevation boundaries or silently relaxing geometry validation.

Stages 2–4 were proposals in the Stage-1 checkpoint. The subsequent reversible implementation and matched checks are documented in [continuous cave terrain](continuous_cave_terrain.md).

## Out-of-bounds ridge scenery (2026-11-13)

`OutOfBoundsScenery` (`scripts/dungeon/out_of_bounds_scenery.gd`) is a renderer child created after the cave background whenever `out_of_bounds_scenery_enabled` (default true) and `cave_scenery_border_width > 0`. It places the three groupmate-authored ridge meshes from `assets/environment/out_of_bounds/oob_{1,2,3}.obj` on 2x2 blocks over the detailed external border and on hash-thinned 4x4 blocks over the apron (`out_of_bounds_far_density`, default 0.6), never over the logical map. Block grids are aligned flush to the rectangle they surround. With `out_of_bounds_foreground_clear` (default true) no block extends south of `map_bounds`: the fixed 35-degree camera follows from the south, so ridges there (near band up to about 3.2 world units, far band up to about 6) would stand between it and the play area; that band keeps the flat ground and apron. Each instance is centered on its block, rests on the highest ground top under it (external record tops or the apron height), is quarter-turned by coordinate hash and vertically stretched by `out_of_bounds_height_scale` (default 2.2). Far blocks reach at most `out_of_bounds_far_extent` (default 16) cells beyond the detailed border, independent of the camera-derived apron guard. One MultiMesh per 16-cell chunk and model (44 nodes on the standard 42x32 layout, about 490k triangles) lets off-screen ridges cull; all share one `shaders/out_of_bounds_spring.gdshader` material: rock on slopes, spring grass on level faces, sparse blossom speckles, no shadow casting. `apply_shade()` follows the border fog palette lifted toward white by `out_of_bounds_shade_lift` (default 0.65). No tiles, elevations, spawns, traversal or generator RNG change; `tests/validate_out_of_bounds_scenery.gd` owns the placement, determinism, shade and toggle contracts. Appearance, draw cost and far-blur interaction need a normal-render check.

### Trees and bushes (2026-11-18)

The same node adds the groupmate's Tinkercad tree and bush models (`assets/environment/out_of_bounds/vegetation/`, files unchanged). Tinkercad exports Z-up millimetres; placement rotates them upright with `z_up_basis()` and scales them to world height. Each ridge model caches its top-surface points: the top vertex nearest the center of each interior cell of a 6x6 grid over its footprint, excluding the base slab, sorted by height. Vegetation picks from the lower half of those points so trees grow in saddles rather than on spikes.

| Setting (DungeonRenderer, Out-of-bounds scenery group) | Default | Effect |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `out_of_bounds_vegetation_enabled` | true | All trees and bushes on/off; ridges unchanged |
| `out_of_bounds_tree_density` | 0.3 | Chance of one tree per ridge block, near and far bands |
| `out_of_bounds_bush_density` | 0.45 | Chance of a bush per near-band block; a second at 40% of this |
| `out_of_bounds_tree_height` | 2.6 | Average tree height in tiles, +/-20%; bushes are 30% |
| `out_of_bounds_blossom_ratio` | 0.3 | Share of trees with pink canopies |

An item is skipped when its canopy radius would reach over the logical map rectangle. The tree's trunk surface keeps a brown base color; canopies and bushes take a per-instance color from three spring greens or blossom pink. Both use `shaders/out_of_bounds_vegetation.gdshader`, which multiplies the ridges' lifted fog shade and brightens sky-facing faces slightly. Duplicated meshes keep the importer's generated LODs. On the standard layout this is 78 trees and 115 bushes, about 610k triangles in 22 chunked MultiMesh nodes, with no shadow casting.
